1. Summary

The original brief described the brand position, a six-stage revenue model and two funding paths built around roughly $75K of seed funding. That all still applies. After reading it, the founders asked for three additions: a budget ranked by priority, a list of revenue streams with prices, and projections that show their assumptions.

The Shifa Institute had no income in the past 12 months, so the projections are planning assumptions with no track record behind them.

The founders will have less time. One has about seven months of part-time capacity before law school, one is in medical school, and one may start a doctorate in clinical psychology. The plan relies on volunteers for most roles and pays contractors where consistency matters most: social media, content direction, podcast editing, clinician modules and grant writing.

The founders have decided to form a for-profit LLC, separate from the 501(c)(3), to own and run the platform. Seed money for the platform goes into the LLC. The LLC's income is meant to help fund the nonprofit's other work. Section 5 splits the budget between the two.

The budget covers 24 months in three stages. Each stage has a checkpoint to meet before the next one is funded.

Every cost in this document is an estimate. Each is a range based on a published price guide, or on an estimate with no published source where no guide was found. Each needs a real quote before a funder sees it as a commitment. The spreadsheet shifa-budget-v2.xlsx has a column for confirmed figures.

2. What stays from the original brief

The original brief is attached as original-brief-2026-11.pdf and none of it is withdrawn. Its main points:

  • The position is faith-informed, evidence-based and open to all.
  • The revenue model has six stages: attract, capture, engage, convert, earn, prove. Content and campus events bring people in, email keeps them, supporters become donors and workshop clients, and outcome data wins grants that pay for more content.
  • There are nine revenue streams, from foundation grants to merchandise, each with a Year 3 target.
  • There are two funding paths. Path A assumes about $75K in seed funding and a first hire in the first quarter. Path B runs on volunteers and free tools and hires only at set checkpoints.
  • Six decisions are waiting on the founders: brand position, funding path, financial records, stage owners, independent board members, and a scholar's opinion on zakat.

Section 8 keeps Path B's checkpoints and extends them. Section 4 lists the original budget lines that are left out of this version.

5. The nonprofit and the for-profit LLC

In November 2026 the founders decided to form a for-profit LLC, separate from the 501(c)(3). The LLC will own and run the platform: paid courses and modules, a directory of therapists who serve Muslim and minority clients, and possibly video sessions in the long term. The nonprofit keeps the community work: chapters, events, the podcast and free education. The founders intend the company's income to fund the nonprofit's other goals.

The LLC receives the seed money for the platform. That money is not a donation to the nonprofit, and the person giving it cannot deduct it. The nonprofit has received no donations so far and this brief does not count on any. What seed funders get back (ownership, repayment, or nothing) is set in the LLC's paperwork with a lawyer. This brief promises no return.

9. Brand rules

A platform, a directory, a podcast, courses, five chapters and possibly a second company all sit under one name. These rules come from the brand kit.

  • The name is always "The Shifa Institute", in full, because other organizations use "Shifa". Chapters are student groups under it and appear as a supporting row, never as the lead.
  • Shifa means healing. Mental health is introduced through culture, curiosity and community first and clinical language second. An initiative that cannot be described that way does not belong under this name.
  • The Shifa Institute provides education and connection. It does not provide treatment, and that statement stays on every page, course and episode until clinical services exist under proper license.
  • The voice is warm and plain-spoken. The look is navy, periwinkle and the arch window. Every statistic, ayah and hadith carries a source, and anything about distress carries a support line (campus counseling or 988).
  • The content director in priority 3 enforces these rules across the podcast, social accounts, platform and chapters.
  • Whether the LLC carries the Shifa name is both a brand question and a legal one, because a donor, a therapist and a customer must be able to tell the two entities apart. It is listed in section 11.

The original brief proposed adding "Muslim & Minority Mental Health" to the name and renaming chapters in the style of "Shifa at MSU". The brand kit completed since then keeps "The Shifa Institute" and the existing chapter names (Spartan Shifa, Dearborn Shifa and so on), and this version follows the brand kit.
